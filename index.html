The Impact of Your Vote

The Beauty of Democracy

The United States of America is a representative democracy. What doees that mean for us, the people of the nation? It means that while people like senators or state represenatives are the ones voting public policies, ordinary people like you and me have the power to elect them and hold them accountable. The government of the US runs on popular sovereignty; on the consent of the people.

The Crisis of Low Voter Turnout

In the 2020 presidential election, 66.8% of eligible voters cast their ballots. That means that 33.2% of eligible voters did not vote. Young voters were especially less likely to vote. These voting outcomes can be tied directly to low voter efficacy. The lower your voter efficacy is, the less you think your vote matters. If you are an eligible voter, you have the power to make a difference in your community and your country by voting.

The Power of Your Voice

No matter how ineffective you think you vote is, every vote counts, and every voice matters. By participating in the American democratic process, you can help elect officials that hold the beliefs that are important to you. In doing so,you can help shape the future of your community and your country. Your vote is a powerful tool for change.

Ways you can help out:

  • Volunteer for campaigns
  • Attend town halls and public meetings
  • Share information about voting rights and responsibilities
